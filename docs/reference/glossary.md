# Glossary

### Incremental reading

A way of learning from texts by reading them in small pieces over many sessions, extracting the important parts, refining them, and turning them into flashcards. The idea comes from [SuperMemo](https://help.supermemo.org/wiki/Incremental_reading).

### Item

Anything in your queue: an article, a snippet, or a card.

### Article

A whole note you've imported, such as a clipped web page or your notes on a book. See [Importing material](/guide/importing).

### Snippet

A passage you've extracted from an article or any other note. Each snippet is its own note, and you revise it over time until it's ready to become a card.

### Card

A fill-in-the-blank flashcard (also called a cloze deletion) scheduled with spaced repetition. See [Make cards](/guide/review-sessions#make-cards).

### Review

Any time an item is shown to you, including the first time you see it.

### Source

The note an article was imported from, or the note a snippet or card was made from. Notes you make snippets from get an `ir-source` tag.

### Parent

The item a snippet or card was made from. For example, a snippet extracted from an article has that article as its parent.

### Priority

A number from 1 to 5 that controls how quickly the time between an article's or snippet's reviews grows. Lower numbers mean more frequent reviews. See [Priority scheduling](/guide/scheduling#priority-scheduling).

### Fixed interval

An alternative to priority: the item comes back every set number of days. See [Fixed-interval scheduling](/guide/scheduling#fixed-interval-scheduling).

### Dismiss

Takes an item out of the queue without deleting it. Dismissed items can be restored.

### Skip

Moves past an item in a review session without reviewing it. The item comes back on the next review day.

### Grade

Your rating of how well you recalled a card: Forgot (Again), Hard, Good, or Easy. See [Grade cards](/guide/review-sessions#grade-cards).

### SRS

Spaced repetition system: software that schedules reviews at growing intervals, so you review things just before you'd forget them. Anki and SuperMemo are well-known examples.

### FSRS

Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler, the [algorithm](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/The-Algorithm) this plugin uses to schedule cards.

### Self-contained

Understandable on its own, without the surrounding text. Cards need to be self-contained, with a clear prompt and only one right answer, for spaced repetition to work well.

### Atomic

Can't be made simpler without losing meaning. Cards should be as atomic as possible. See [atomic memory](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Atomic_memory).
