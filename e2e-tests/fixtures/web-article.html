Gardens of the Northern Coast

The gardens along the northern coast grow in thin soil over granite, shaped by salt wind for most of the year. Gardeners there plant in sheltered pockets behind stone walls, where the air is still enough for seedlings to take root before the autumn storms arrive.

Hardy perennials do best: sea thrift, lavender and rosemary all tolerate the spray, and their roots hold the soil in place through the winter. Annuals are grown in raised beds filled with compost carted in from the valleys inland, since the native ground holds too little water for them.

Many of the oldest gardens were laid out by lighthouse keepers, who had long evenings and little else to tend. Their walled plots still stand beside the towers, and some are kept by volunteers who replant them each spring from seed saved the year before.
